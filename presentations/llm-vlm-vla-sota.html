From words to actions
LLM → VLM → VLA

Three families of foundation models, one difference that matters: what comes out of the model. Text, understanding of a scene, or motor commands that move a robot — plus a researched survey of the frontier of each family.

＋🦾
VLA Vision-Language-Action image + text + state in → actions out Sees, understands and acts. Closes the loop with the physical world.

The one-slide answer

Nested capabilities, not competing technologies

LLM

Large Language Model. Transformer trained on trillions of text tokens to predict the next token.

Output

"pick → place → verify"

Role in robotics
  • Task planner: goal → ordered sub-goals
  • Code generator: Code as Policies
  • Symbolic bridge: NL → PDDL (LLM+P)
Blind spot

No eyes, no physics, no closed loop. Cannot see the cup it is talking about.

VLM

Vision-Language Model. An LLM plus a vision encoder and alignment training on image–text pairs.

Output

"red cube at (0.42, 0.18, 0.05) m"

Role in robotics
  • Semantic perception: what & where
  • Grounding / pointing: language → pixels → 3D
  • Failure diagnosis: why did it fail?
Blind spot

Still answers with text. Needs a classical controller (MoveIt 2, Nav2) to move.

VLA

Vision-Language-Action. A VLM backbone plus an action head trained on robot trajectories.

Output

Δx, Δy, Δz, Δrpy, gripper @ 10–50 Hz

Role in robotics
  • End-to-end policy: perception → control
  • Closed loop: re-decides every frame
  • Cross-embodiment: one model, many robots
Blind spot

Data-hungry (teleoperation), latency-bound, hard to certify and to audit.

Key difference: a VLM responds with text; a VLA responds with physical actions. An LLM responds with text and has never seen an image. Everything else — scale, pretraining, reasoning — is shared inheritance.

Part 2 · Large Language Models

LLM — the reasoning layer

What changed by 2026

  • Reasoning models are the default. A model can spend thousands of "thinking" tokens before answering; test-time compute is now a tunable knob, not a curiosity.
  • Agentic behaviour. Tool calling, multi-step loops, self-verification and long-horizon coding agents (SWE-bench-style) are the headline benchmark, not chat quality.
  • Context at million-token scale. 1M-token windows are common in the top tier; the largest published window is 2M tokens (Grok-4.1 Fast).
  • Open weights closed the gap. Kimi K3, GLM-5.3 and Qwen3.8 Max sit inside the frontier band on reasoning benchmarks at a fraction of the price.
  • Price collapse. Frontier-class reasoning is now available from $0.02 / 1M input tokens (Gemma 4 E4B) to ~$12 / 1M for the top closed models.

What an LLM is not

  • Not grounded. It has never observed the scene; it reasons about a description of it.
  • Not physical. No dynamics, no friction, no contact — it cannot predict whether a grasp will slip.
  • Not real-time. Seconds per answer (more with thinking) vs. milliseconds required by a control loop.
  • Not closed-loop. It emits a plan once; it does not re-decide at 30 Hz as the world changes.
Mental model

An LLM is a symbolic engine: it manipulates descriptions of the world extremely well, and it is the best available task decomposer and code writer for robotics.

The constant: in every one of these patterns the LLM sits above the control loop. The 50–500 Hz servo, the collision checking and the force limits are still classical, deterministic software.

Why it works

  • Open-vocabulary task decomposition, zero shot
  • Common-sense priors humans take for granted
  • Explains its own plan — auditable dialogue
  • Re-plans from text feedback cheaply

Where it breaks

  • Hallucinated capabilities: plans skills the robot does not have
  • No metric grounding: "the red cup" → which pixel, which 3D point?
  • Latency: seconds per decision, worse with reasoning enabled
  • Uncertainty is poorly calibrated for safety-critical gating
2026 status: closed-loop LLM planners now ship with uncertainty-based failure detection (see 2405.xxxx UQ for planners in papers.csv), but recovery — not just detection — remains an open gap in research-gaps.md.
Part 3 · Vision-Language Models

VLM — the perception layer

What a VLM adds over an LLM

  • Grounding. "The mug left of the laptop" resolves to a specific region — bounding boxes, points, masks.
  • Scene semantics. Objects, states (open/closed, full/empty), relations, occlusion, human activity.
  • Document & OCR fluency. Labels, screens, warning signs, serial numbers — useful in industrial cells.
  • Spatial reasoning. Depth ordering, relative position, free-space for placement; still the weakest axis in 2026.
  • Visual verification. "Did the grasp succeed?" answered from a post-action image.

Three ways to build one

  • Encoder + projector + frozen/finetuned LLM — the classic recipe (LLaVA-style, Florence-2, Qwen-VL family).
  • Natively multimodal — vision tokens are part of pretraining from the start (Gemini, GPT-5.x, Claude).
  • Contrastive dual-encoder — CLIP-style embeddings; no generation, but excellent retrieval and zero-shot classification.

What a VLM still cannot do

  • Emit motor commands. Output is text; a controller must convert it into motion.
  • Guarantee metric accuracy. Pointing is good, not metrology; depth is inferred, not measured.
  • Feel contact. Force, slip and friction are invisible to cameras — the case for tactile (V-T-L-A).
  • Run at control rate. Even "flash" tiers answer in hundreds of milliseconds.

Part 4 · Vision-Language-Action

VLA — the layer that moves the robot

1 Action representation

  • Discrete tokens — bin each dimension into 256 buckets and reuse the LLM vocabulary (RT-2, OpenVLA). Simple, but slow and lossy.
  • Continuous chunks — regress k future steps directly (OpenVLA-OFT with L1, Diffusion Policy, ACT). Fast, precise.
  • Diffusion / flow matching — denoise a trajectory from noise conditioned on the VLM embedding (π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.x, RDT). Multimodal action distributions.
  • Discrete diffusion — the 2026 research favourite: denoise tokens, not vectors.
  • Latent actions — learn an action space from video, no teleoperation labels needed.

2 Decoding & frequency

  • Action chunking: predict k steps per inference, execute open-loop for a few of them → amortises latency.
  • Parallel decoding: emit the whole chunk at once instead of token by token (26× faster action generation in OFT).
  • Dual-system: a slow VLM (System 2, ~1–5 Hz) steers a fast reactive policy (System 1, 20–50 Hz) — GR00T N1.x, Gemini Robotics 1.5.
  • Async inference: the policy keeps acting on the previous chunk while the next one is computed (SmolVLA).
Budget

<10 ms reactive · 10–50 ms standard control · 50–200 ms acceptable planning · >200 ms only non-urgent decisions.

3 Where the knowledge comes from

  • Internet-scale VLM pretraining — inherited semantics and reasoning (the definitional ingredient).
  • Cross-embodiment robot data — Open X-Embodiment (22+ robots), AgiBot World, lab-collected teleoperation.
  • Human video — GR00T N1.7 uses 20,000 h of egocentric human video (EgoScale) plus simulator data.
  • Motion Transfer — Gemini Robotics 1.5 maps heterogeneous embodiments into one representation space.
  • RL from the robot's own experience — π*0.6 / RECAP (2025-11).
Three consequences of adding "A": (1) the metric becomes success rate on a physical task, not perplexity; (2) latency becomes a design constraint, not an afterthought; (3) the training data becomes scarce and expensive — you cannot scrape robot trajectories from the internet.

Part 4 · The reliability jump

π*0.6 & RECAP: imitation is not enough

RECAP — RL with Experience & Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies

1 · Demonstrationsteleoperation → defines new behaviour
→
2 · Offline RLvalue pretraining on all collected data
→
3 · Correctionsan expert coaches mid-episode
→
4 · Autonomous experiencethe robot practises; advantage conditions the policy

The policy is conditioned on an advantage estimate, so at deployment it can be told "behave like the best version of yourself" — and it keeps improving as it accumulates its own rollouts.

Espresso

Made drinks continuously from 5:30 am to 11:30 pm — long horizon, pouring liquids, waiting for the grinder, cleaning the machine with a cloth.

Laundry

Folded 50 novel items in a new home, for hours without interruption — different garments, different fabric dynamics.

Factory boxes

Assembled and labelled 59 boxes used for real chocolate packaging, handling stuck blanks and re-folding mistakes.

Reported effect of RECAP over the π0.6 pretrain baseline: more than 2× throughput on the hardest tasks and failure rates reduced 2× or more, with >90% success on each stage of the espresso task. Ablations show offline RL alone, or offline RL + SFT, do not reach the same level.

Why imitation stalls

A behaviour-cloned policy inherits the demonstrator's distribution. Its own small errors push it out of distribution, where it has no supervision — compounding failure. This is the classic covariate-shift problem; RECAP fixes it by training on the policy's own states.

The strategic claim

Demonstrations define behaviour, coaching refines strategy, autonomous experience perfects it — and experience may become the largest data source, potentially enabling superhuman speed. This is the 2026 answer to the data bottleneck.

Source: Physical Intelligence, "π*0.6: a VLA that Learns from Experience", published 2025-11-17 (physicalintelligence.company/blog/pistar06).

Apollo 2 humanoid (Inspire / SharpaWave 22-DoF hands) and Franka Duo. Google DeepMind internal evaluations, no technical report published, no external replication.

What is new
  • One policy for the whole body: legs, torso, arms, multi-finger hands
  • 22-DoF five-finger hands — beyond the parallel grippers of earlier generations
  • Embodiment range: from SO-101 tabletop arms to humanoids
  • Demonstrated walking to a table, picking a watering can, stepping to a shelf and placing it
What the failures reveal
  • Asymmetric dexterity: unscrewing 92% vs screwing 36% — the "easy" direction is learned, the precision one is not
  • Balance-critical manipulation: floor pick 45.7% because it requires squatting and balance recovery
  • Deformables & bimanual coordination: trash bag 44%
  • Closed weights, waitlist access, no published params / latency / control frequency
The honest reading

Whole-body control is now demonstrated, not solved. The gap between 92% and 36% on the same object, in opposite directions, is the clearest available measure of where dexterity stands in 2026.

Partners named

Apptronik (Apollo 2), Franka (Duo), Agile Robots — plus Trossen / Dexmate / SO-101 class hardware for the low end of the embodiment range.

Compare with the open ecosystem: NVIDIA GR00T N1.7 ships whole-upper-body humanoid control under an open licence with published benchmarks — the openness gap, not the capability gap, is what most teams should weigh.
